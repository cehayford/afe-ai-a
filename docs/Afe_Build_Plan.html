1 · SummaryThe plan in one screen

Afe helps renters in Accra find homes without getting scammed, by verifying every landlord and property and holding rent payments safely until move-in. AI strengthens detection on top of verified data; it is not a substitute for verification.

The Project Statement (v3.3) plans a large Release 1: Accra and Kumasi, native Android and iOS apps, a Rust module, Typesense, escrow and GPS attestation, all shipping together around week 28. The brief asks for something smaller that we can start sooner. We verify by hand first, list only verified homes, and turn on protected payments after that works. AI comes last, and it learns from what the first two phases record.

This plan follows the brief. We put a small verified-listings product in front of renters by early January 2027. We open protected payments to everyone around the same week-28 mark the Statement aimed for. By then we will have had three months of real listings, verification cost data and fee revenue.

W14Public in 2–3 Accra neighbourhoods (4 Jan 2027)

Launch offer: free for the first 8 months

Every Afe charge (the verification fee, the protection fee and featured listings) is free from W1 (5 Oct 2026) until 5 Jun 2027. The end date is a rule, so it moves with two reviewers, not a deploy. Charges start at the end of W35. How they're switched on, and what the offer costs, is in the Feature Roadmap.

What makes this plan work

Afe's own field verifier takes the listing photos. Photos shot by Afe during the physical visit become the only photos on the listing. For verified homes, that shuts down copied photos at the source. It also gives the duplicate-photo index a clean, trusted set of photos to compare against.

One physical home is one record. A registry of units, not of listings, is what stops the same room being let twice.

A free "Check before you pay" lookup on WhatsApp. Renters send us a phone number or listing, and we tell them whether Afe has verified it. It brings in renters before we have many listings. It also gives Phase 3 its fraud examples, because verified listings should produce almost none.

Where the build differs from this plan

  • Database, a requirement: development runs on SQLite for now. Beta (staging) and production must run on PostgreSQL. The app enforces this: with debug off, it refuses to start on anything but PostgreSQL. The local Docker stack and CI already use PostgreSQL, and the Terraform for staging provisions RDS PostgreSQL 16.
  • Web client: a separate SvelteKit app instead of server-rendered templates (B3, decided). The API has no pages of its own, so the web app and any future phone app use the same endpoints. §2 and §8 are updated.
  • Background jobs: no Celery or Redis yet. Scheduled work runs as commands (run-due every few minutes, reconcile nightly, publish-outbox every minute) as scheduled ECS tasks. Money events go to the Postgres outbox first, as planned. Add a queue when volume needs one.
  • Maps and search: coordinates and area boundaries are plain columns, so the same code runs on SQLite in development. PostGIS is added on PostgreSQL when geo queries need it. Search filters by area, bedrooms and rent.
  • Data model names: users instead of parties; tenancies plus ledger_entries instead of protected_payments / payment_events. Reviewer decisions and reason codes are stored on each verification. Phase 3 added detection_labels, model_scores and the link_edges account graph. Linking by shared device uses a random per-browser ID, and leaves staff accounts out.
  • LLM scoring: off by default. Before turning on the Claude classifier, check with counsel that sending redacted message text to a processor abroad is allowed (Act 843, B11). Phone numbers, emails and long numbers are masked before sending.
  • Upload safety: photos are re-encoded as WebP with all metadata removed. Documents are scanned by ClamAV before they're stored (a container beside the API in staging), and uploads are refused if the scanner is down. They go to private storage that only reviewers can read.
  • Hardening added after Phase 3: rate limits on "Check before you pay", scam reports and WhatsApp lookups; a random device ID per browser for account links; PDF receipts; and unannounced re-checks of 15% of approved homes by a second verifier, with reversal rates per verifier (§5). It also fixed the partner's confirmation address, which was missing from the load balancer rule.
  • B8 and B9 have working defaults held as rules, so they can change without a deploy. B8: release 7 days after move-in when the landlord has recorded the handover and nobody disputes it. B9: a 3.5% fee paid by the tenant.

3 · PrinciplesBuild rules

Eight rules that settle arguments before they start. Each one comes from the brief or survives unchanged from the Statement.

Verification is a record, not a checkbox

Every "verified" state points to evidence, who decided, when, and when it expires. Nothing is ever just is_verified = true.

One home, one record

Units are registered once, keyed by GhanaPost digital address and unit label. A listing is an offer on a unit, and a unit can have only one live listing.

Afe never holds the money

Funds sit with a licensed partner. Afe's ledger records instructions and states. It never holds a balance.

The back office is the product

In Phase 1, reviewers and field verifiers do the verifying. Their tools get the same care as the renter's screens.

Every flag explains itself

Renters see what was checked and when. Reviewers see which rule fired and why. No unexplained scores.

The law is configuration

Advance-rent caps, fees and time windows live in versioned rules with dual approval, not in code.

AI runs in shadow first

Every model starts by logging scores next to human decisions. It gets to act only after it beats the rules on held-out data.

Humans make the final call

No automated system bans a person or keeps someone's money without a named human decision.

What the renter sees

Every listing shows a verification panel in plain words, for example: "Landlord's Ghana Card checked 12 Nov 2026 · Indenture sighted · Owner confirmed by phone · Visited by Afe on 14 Nov 2026 · Photos taken by Afe." The panel is the product, and it's also our best marketing.

Controls on our own verifiers

In a manual system, a verifier who takes a bribe is the weakest point. Controls from day one:

  • Verifiers capture photos and GPS in the Afe verifier web app. Files uploaded from elsewhere aren't accepted.
  • A second verifier re-checks a random 15% of approvals, unannounced.
  • Verifiers rotate between neighbourhoods. The same verifier can't approve the same landlord twice in a row.
  • Every decision goes to the append-only audit log. Verifier approval rates and reversal rates are reviewed monthly.

Picking the neighbourhoods (by W3)

Score candidates on: rental volume, renter mix (students, young professionals, families), how far apart homes are (short travel between visits keeps verification cheap), and how often people there report scams in the survey. Candidates to test: East Legon, Madina/Adenta (near the universities), Spintex, Dzorwulu/Airport Residential, Achimota. Pick two to start and add a third at W40.

Capacity is the real limit

Assumption to test in W1–4: one verifier does 2–3 visits a day in a compact area, so 10–15 a week. Two verifiers from W5 give roughly 190 visits by W14. At a 70% pass rate that is about 130 verified homes. The year-one listing target follows from verifier headcount, not from how many landlords sign up.

6 · Phase 2 coreProtected payments

The brief's rule: rent is held with a licensed payment partner and released to the landlord when the tenant confirms move-in. Engineering on this can't start until the legal structure is settled (gate at W8).

Legal gate · decision B7 · needed by W8

Holding rent for other people is regulated by the Bank of Ghana under the Payment Systems and Services Act, 2019 (Act 987). Get a written opinion from counsel. It should choose between (a) a licensed payment company's held or delayed-settlement product, (b) an escrow or trust account at a bank where Afe only gives instructions, and (c) a licensed e-money partner's wallet in the tenant's name. Paystack and Hubtel are the brief's candidates. Ask each in writing whether they support conditional release and refunds on mobile money.

Non-negotiables for the money path

  • Caps come from rules. The advance-rent limit is checked when a tenancy is offered. Act 220 caps advance rent at six months for tenancies over six months. The brief itself says tenants are often asked for one to two years, so this affects how many landlords will list. Counsel must confirm how Afe applies it (B10).
  • Ledger plus outbox. Every state change writes an append-only payment event and an outbox row in the same database transaction. Partner webhooks are signature-checked and idempotent. A nightly job reconciles our ledger against the partner's statements.
  • The payee is verified. Payouts go only to a MoMo or bank account whose name matches the verified owner or authorised agent. Changing the payout account means MFA, re-verification and a 72-hour cooling-off period, which blocks account-takeover payouts.
  • Idempotency keys on every payment call. Mobile money is only ever handled through the partner's hosted checkout, so no card data reaches us.
  • Pilot limits. From W24–27, cap the amount per tenancy and the total held. The founder or trust lead reviews every release.

Where the training data comes from

If verification works, verified listings produce almost no fraud to learn from. Our fraud examples will come from three places: rejected applications (each with a reason code), reports through the "Check before you pay" lookup, and scam messages people forward to us. Every reviewer decision is stored as a label from day one, using a fixed list of reason codes.

When a model gets promoted: it must beat the rules on precision and recall on a held-out labelled set, at the same reviewer workload. It then runs as a 10% canary. We never judge a fraud model on accuracy, because a model that flags nothing still scores 99%.

11 · DeliveryPhase-by-phase build

Two-week sprints. Each phase ends with a gate. If the exit criteria aren't met, the next phase doesn't start, and we fix the gap rather than move the date.

Phase 0 · Ground truth

W1–W4 · 5–30 Oct 2026 · runs alongside Sprint 0–1

Before building the software, prove the verification process works by hand.

  • Run the two validation surveys (already built in afe_survey_forms.gs). Aim for 150+ renters and 40+ landlords and agents in the candidate neighbourhoods.
  • Interview 30 landlords and agents. Sign letters of intent with 50 "founding landlords".
  • Verify 10 homes by hand, using a spreadsheet and WhatsApp. Time each step, cost each visit, write SOP v1 and the reason-code list.
  • Manual rent survey of about 200 asking prices per neighbourhood and bedroom count, to set the first price bands.
  • Engage counsel with four questions: escrow structure (Act 987), how to apply the advance-rent cap (Act 220), wording for the scam-check lookup (defamation risk), and data protection registration and hosting abroad (Act 843). File the Data Protection Commission registration.

14 · ViabilityMoney

The unit economics workbook uses placeholder inputs and currently shows cash running out in month 5. That model assumes the Statement's plan: a larger team and no revenue until week 28. The brief's order changes two inputs in our favour. Verification fees start in W10–14. The Phase 1 team is roughly half the size.

The launch offer changes this again: no fee revenue until 5 Jun 2027 (the end of W35). Afe pays for every verification visit and the gateway's charge on every protected payment until then. Model the offer explicitly, and move break-even to after W35.

Three things the workbook needs before anyone relies on it:

  • Add the verification fee as a revenue line, with the cost of a visit (verifier time plus transport) as its direct cost. Phase 0 measures both.
  • The gateway fee is charged on the whole amount held, not on our fee. At the placeholder 1.8% on GHS 14,000, the gateway takes GHS 252 of a GHS 490 protection fee. Negotiate a capped or tiered rate as part of the partner contract.
  • Leakage is the number to watch. A 3.5% fee is about a quarter of a month's rent. Survey question 19 (the "300 cedis off for cash" question) and the pilot's measured leakage decide the fee level and who pays it (B9).

17 · GrowthExpansion ladder

Start with a few neighbourhoods, then widen one step at a time. The code can handle many countries from the start, but each new area opens only after the one before it meets its targets.

Why not a whole country at once

Every home is checked in person, so the distance between homes drives the cost and speed of each check. Renters also need many verified homes close together before the site is useful: 300 homes in two neighbourhoods is a working marketplace, while 300 spread across Ghana is empty everywhere. And "near-zero fraud" can only be proven where we control the checks closely.

Why a country isn't just a bigger neighbourhood

Each country has its own rent law, data protection rules, ID system (Ghana Card vs Nigeria's NIN), mobile-money providers and rules on holding money. Opening a new neighbourhood means hiring verifiers. Opening a new country means a legal opinion, a payment partner and a KYC partner first.

Built now so each step is cheap later

  • Every record carries a country code. Currency, time zone and language are set per country.
  • Laws are stored as versioned rules (compliance_rules), not written into the code.
  • KYC and payment providers sit behind one interface, so adding a country means writing a new connector, not rewriting the app.
  • Each area Afe covers is its own record (service_areas), switched on one at a time. Search and verifier routing respect those boundaries.
  • Not built yet: separate deployments per country, hosting in several regions, or languages beyond English and Twi.
